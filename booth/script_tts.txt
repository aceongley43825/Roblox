In this town, your sixteenth birthday is the most important day of your life. Because when you turn sixteen, you get one phone call from your future self. But the call can only happen inside the old red phone booth in the middle of the town square. It's been standing there for over two hundred years, and nobody knows who built it. Nobody is allowed to touch it before they turn sixteen. And every year, the whole town comes to watch. My best friend Milo went last spring. His future self told him to never buy a blue car, so now he rides a bike everywhere. My grandma's future self told her to marry the boy who sold ice cream. That's my grandpa. And everybody always says the same thing about the booth. It's tiny. Milo got stuck in the door for twenty minutes. Then it was my turn. The night of my birthday, the whole town was standing in the square, holding candles. I squeezed inside, closed the door, and waited. The phone rang. I picked it up. A voice said, "Hey. It's you. I'm calling from thirty years in the future, and I don't have much time." Everyone outside went completely silent. Then he said, "In a few years, you're going to build a time machine. You're going to travel back two hundred years, and you're going to build a phone booth. Please, whatever you do, make the door bigger." Then the line went dead. So now I know who built the booth. It was me. And I still haven't figured out how to get out of it.
